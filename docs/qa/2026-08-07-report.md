## Is it the model? (the question that matters for rollback)

I ran a controlled comparison to separate "new model broke it" from "already broken".

**Menemen appears in both plan #134 (generated 2026-08-05 on `gemini-2.5-flash`) and
plan #138 (today, on `gemini-3.5-flash`).** Its macros are byte-identical in both:

```
FAT 34g · CARBS 28g · PROTEIN 22g · CALORIES 494
```

Recipe prose, descriptions and nutrition are served from the curated corpus, not
generated per-plan. The model's actual job is **slot selection** (which recipe fills
which slot) plus the **refine chat**. Both of those behaved well on 3.5.

Corroborating: the worst nutrition figure I saw is in the **old-model** plan #134 —
*Špenát s bramborovým knedlíkem* claiming **46 g protein** from 300 g frozen spinach,
53 ml milk and half an egg. That is more inflated than anything 3.5 produced today.

**Conclusion: the 2.5→3.5 swap did not degrade grounding, coherence, portioning or
Czech copy.** The defects below predate it.

## Failure detail

### D1 — Nutrition labelled "na porci" is actually the whole-recipe total (HIGH, pre-existing)

Every corpus recipe I checked shows per-portion macros that equal the *whole recipe's*
totals, roughly 2x overstated.

*Domácí bramborové halušky* — ingredient list for **2 porce**:
`300 g brambory, 93 g polohrubá mouka, 0,5 lžičky solamyl, 0,5 ks vejce`

Displayed: `NUTRIČNÍ HODNOTY · na porci — ENERGIE 616 kcal, BÍLKOVINY 20 g, SACHARIDY 119 g, TUKY 5 g`

Computed whole recipe: 300 g potato ≈ 231 kcal + 93 g flour ≈ 339 kcal + half an egg
≈ 36 kcal = **~606 kcal, ~19 g protein**. That matches the "per portion" figure almost
exactly. True per-portion is ~303 kcal / ~10 g protein.

Same pattern on *Citronová cizrnová polévka* (claims 699 kcal / 39 g fat per portion;
ingredients support ~324 kcal / ~14 g fat per portion).

Why it matters: this is the core nutritional promise of a diet product. A user cutting
calories is being told they ate ~2x what they actually did. The headline
`PRŮM. KCAL/DEN 1679` is correspondingly inflated.

Not a deploy regression — needs its own corpus/ETL fix.

### D2 — Scaled ingredients vs unscaled step prose (MEDIUM, pre-existing)

*Citronová cizrnová polévka*, assigned 3 portions. The ingredient list is scaled;
the prose steps retain the source recipe's 4-portion numbers:

| Ingredient list (3 porce) | Step prose says |
|---|---|
| `1,5 lžíce olivový olej` | "rozehřejte **2 lžíce** olivového oleje" |
| `371 g vařená cizrna` | "**275 g** vařené cizrny" + "**220 g** cizrny" = 495 g |
| `720 ml zeleninový vývar` | "přilijte **720 ml**" then "zbývajících **240 ml**" = 960 ml |
| `45 g čerstvý špenát` | "Přidejte **60 g** čerstvého špenátu" |
| `1,5 lžíce tahini` | "**2 lžíce** tahini" |

Step 4 also says "Polévku rozdělte do **čtyř** misek" on a 3-portion recipe.

Confirmed mechanism: clicking "Více porcí" to 4 makes the ingredient list read exactly
`2 lžíce` / `495 g` — i.e. the prose is static text written for the source recipe's
native 4-portion yield, and only the ingredient list scales. Any plan whose serving
count ≠ the source recipe's native yield shows contradictory quantities.

2150 kcal reads as an absurd single meal and would make a user reject a perfectly good
recipe. After accepting it, the recipe page correctly shows **538 kcal**. 2150 / 4
portions = 537.5 — the card is showing the recipe total against a per-meal label.
